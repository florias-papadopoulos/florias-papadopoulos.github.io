How it works
  1. Partitioner (Section 3). Take the bounding box of the region, cut it into cells of r × r lattice points, keep the cells that lie wholly inside, and fuse neighbours that share a whole side until none do. The Nr rectangles and the Lr points of the region they miss (the residual points) together hold every lattice point of the region exactly once.
  2. Cost (Section 4). Each rectangle becomes its own small ℓ∞ test, which costs Bob work for every one of them; a residual point costs Alice one entry. Counting the protocol's steps: Cost(r) = Σk,i 2⌊log2(2δk,i+1)⌋ + Lr + (d+1)·Nr·JS,   JS = Πi(⌊log2(2δmax,i+1)⌋+1) that is, Alice's padded strings per rectangle side, her residual points, and the Nr·JS answers Bob computes (d = 2 lookups each) and Alice checks: the terms of the paper's complexity theorem. r* is the r with the smallest cost. The paper gives no explicit formula for the cost; “Best for” lets you minimise both sides’ work (this one), one side’s, or the bytes sent, each read off the same theorem and protocol figure.
  3. DASA (Section 2). Along each axis a rectangle is an interval of 7-bit numbers. Alice writes it as the few prefixes whose subtrees fit wholly inside (the rest of the bits are wildcards *). Bob writes his coordinate with 0, 1, 2, … of its last bits as wildcards, as many as the widest rectangle needs. He is inside a rectangle exactly when one of his strings equals one of Alice's, in x and in y. A residual point is matched as a whole.
  4. Who learns what. Alice learns one bit: inside or not (the answers come shuffled, so not even which rectangle). Bob learns the rectangle sizes he needs for his strings, not where they are, and no answer.

This page shows the geometry in the clear. In the protocol Alice's strings travel inside an oblivious key-value store and Bob answers with re-randomised Diffie-Hellman pairs, so the matching above happens under encryption, in two messages, with no server in between. From I. Katis, K. Mitrokotsa, F. Papadopoulos, Privacy-preserving Proximity Testing from Geometric Fuzzy Matching, ACM ASIA CCS 2026 (ACM Digital Library).
